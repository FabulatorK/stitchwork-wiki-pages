Priority & Conflicts

introplayercreator5 min read
For Stitchwork 0.9.0 · Last edited: 2026-10-16

Ship · everyone.
Two packs change the same sprite: which one wins?
The rule, how to control it, and how to read the COVERED fold in the Pack Manager.

You'll be able to: predict which pack wins a clash, change that outcome by reordering, read the COVERED fold, and recognize the deliberate exceptions to the rule.

The rule, in one line

When two enabled packs target the same asset, the higher-priority pack wins. Everything else on this page is a corollary of that.

Priority is the list order

In the Pack Manager (the Packs panel), packs are listed in priority order, top = highest. The pack nearer the top wins any asset both of them touch. To change who wins, change the order — drag a card up or down, then click Apply. The grip at the left of the card's bottom bar is the drag handle; its tooltip says "The higher pack wins." Nothing settles until Apply. Before Apply, a new order changes only the cards, not the game.

Each card also counts what it loses. Its status line shows, for example, 4 covered by Crystal Hornet (or 4 covered by 2 packs): four of its assets show from a higher pack instead. The count follows the order you have staged, so after a drag the cards show the new outcome before you click Apply. A card with conditions shows its conditions as a sentence on that line instead.

A useful mental model: a clash isn't pack-vs-pack wholesale, it's per asset. Pack X can win the cloak while Pack Y wins the music, if that's how their files fall. Priority only decides the assets they both claim.

Reading the COVERED fold

The Pack Manager doesn't hide collisions — it reports them in the COVERED fold, under the pack list. The header shows none, or the count, such as 3 assets. Click it to open the list. Each row reads <key> shows <winner> over <loser>, so you can see exactly what's being overridden and by whom. (base) in a row means the working folder. Most keys are sprite or texture paths. A sound shows as sound:<clip>, a cinematic as video:<cinematic>, and a scene's ambient light as <scene>/ambient. An Appliqué frame from Clips/ shows as <collection>/<clip>/<sprite>.

Each row has one button:

  • Open shows the losing file in its folder (or opens the losing pack's folder).
  • Resolve replaces Open on a text row where the working folder wins. It opens the Text tab and compares that pack's text with the working folder.

The status badge in the bottom-left corner shows the total too, as N covered, when the count is above 0 — a quick "how tangled is my current setup" gauge without opening anything.

Two image files with the same name and the same bytes don't count as a clash, so an exact copy in two packs isn't listed. For a sound or a video, the same name and the same file size count as one file.

Sheets and individual files across packs

Inside one pack, individual sprite files draw on top of that pack's sheet ("Sheets and individual sprites compose" in Porting Prior Work). That stacking stops at the pack's edge. A sheet replaces a whole material, so it's one asset: when a higher pack ships the sheet, a lower pack's individual files for that material don't show. The COVERED fold lists it as a sheet: row that shows the higher pack. Point at the row: its tooltip says that the higher pack's sheet takes the whole material, and what you can do.

Want your individual fixes over another pack's sheet (a Customizer skin, say)? Move your pack above it and Apply.

When two packs ship a sheet for the same Texture2D page, the higher pack's sheet shows. The file name does not change that: a lower pack's file with the texture's full Unity name loses to a higher pack's file with the clean name.

For sprites, your working folder ranks below every pack. Its individual sprites for a material don't show when an active pack ships the sheet for that material.

Appliqué frames follow the same rule (Appliqué). A Clips/ frame shows over a sheet from its own pack or from a lower pack. When a higher pack ships the sheet for the frame's material, the frame shows that sheet, and the COVERED fold lists the frame. This applies to the Clips/ frames in your working folder too.

The exceptions: what the in-game editors write

Every asset type follows "packs beat the working folder" — except text and recolor rules. For those, the working folder beats every pack.

It's deliberate. The Text tab's Editor panel (in Game text mode) saves to the root Text/, and the Graphics tab's Colors mode saves to the root Recolor/. Root-wins guarantees an edit you just made in an editor is never silently shadowed by an installed pack. So: sprites, T2D, audio, video → packs over root; text and recolor rules → root over packs. (More in Text & Dialogue and Colors.)

"My pack worked until I added another"

Almost always: the new pack overrides the same asset. Open the COVERED fold — it names the exact keys that collide and who's now winning. Your card's "covered by" line names the winning pack too.

  • If both packs may change that asset and you want yours to show, drag your pack above the new one, then Apply.
  • If the new pack shouldn't be touching your asset at all, the cause is in that pack: its files are mis-named, or it changes more than its author intended. Tell its author, because the fix is in their pack.

Layering on purpose

Conflicts aren't only accidents — you can stack packs deliberately:

  • Base + accent. Put a full skin low and a small "just the cloak" pack high; the accent wins its few assets, the base wins the rest.
  • Addon packs. In the Pack Manager you can make one pack apply only while another pack is on (a condition with the subject Pack and the operator is on), so it only applies on top of its parent — see Conditional Activation. The conditions in the drawer belong to the player's install, not to the pack, so an addon's README should tell players which condition to set. A pack can carry its own conditions, for parts of itself, as rule sets (a preview, at the end of Conditional Activation).

Used this way, priority is a layering tool, and the covered count is just the seam where your layers meet.
