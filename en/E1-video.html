E1 Video

introcreator4 min read
For Stitchwork 0.9.1 · Last edited: 2026-10-22

Video · for creators.
A video file with the cutscene's name plays in its place.
The hard part is the encoding.

You'll be able to: replace any cinematic, and encode the replacement so it actually plays back smoothly rather than stuttering or refusing to load.

Before you start: Module A. You need nothing else first: if you can export a video file, you can make a video pack.

What you can replace

The game's cinematics — the pre-rendered cutscenes. When the game plays one, Stitchwork gives it your file in place of the built-in video. Stitchwork does not convert your file: the game's own video player plays it as it is.

The path

The path is inside your pack folder (Pack Folder Structure):

Videos/{CinematicName}.{ext}

The name (no extension) must match the cinematic's internal name. Upper and lower case do not matter, and Stitchwork also looks in subfolders of Videos/.

Find the name in the Video tab (key 5, or its pull tab at the top of the screen). It opens one small panel, Cinematics, at the bottom right:

  • The list shows the game's cinematics. Each row reads replaced (your file is on disk), played (it played this session), or available. Click a row to copy the name.
  • When you drag the list's scrollbar thumb, a tag beside it shows the start of the name of the row in the middle of the list.
  • The Live cinematic log under it lists the cinematics that played, newest first, with the time. A green R chip marks a replaced one. Clear empties the log.

Stitchwork finds a new file after a relaunch or a Pack Manager Apply. While the Cinematics panel is open, Stitchwork also scans the Videos/ folders every 2 seconds, so you do not need an Apply for a new file.

A file whose name matches no cinematic name gets a warning in BepInEx/LogOutput.log:

[VideoHandler] 'name' matches no known cinematic file name. It plays only if it is the name of a cinematic's video clip; else rename the file to the cinematic's name (the Video tab lists them all).

Encoding — the one part that matters

The steps above are short. The encoding decides whether a video plays well. Use these settings, and you'll be fine:

  • Codec: H.264. The game's video player plays it most reliably. With H.264, most playback problems do not occur.
  • Frame rate: 30 FPS is the recommended rate.
  • Container: .mp4. Use it first. Stitchwork also finds files with these extensions: .webm, .ogv, .mov, .avi, .m4v, .mpg, .mpeg and .wmv.
  • Bitrate: moderate. A video with a very high bitrate is harder to decode, and it can stutter, more so when heavy packs are active. Use a bitrate that suits the video's resolution. The video does not need a very high bitrate to look good in motion.

In short: H.264, 30 FPS, MP4, a moderate bitrate. With a different codec, 60 FPS or a very high bitrate, a playback problem almost certainly comes from the encode. Try the settings above first.

Sound and playback options

With the default settings, the sound of a replaced cinematic uses the game's volume settings and follows the game's fade-outs.

Three switches change playback. They are in Settings › Video: press 0, or click the gear tab at the right end of the pull tabs. Each switch is also a key in the [Video] section of the config file.

  • Drop frames to stay in sync (VideoSkipFrameOnDrop, default on) — when the frame rate dips, the video skips frames to stay in sync. When it is off, the video slows down instead, and you hear the audio chop. This applies to every cinematic, replaced or not.
  • Show replaced videos as made (VideoSkipColorGrading, default on) — a replaced cinematic shows without the game's color grade, so it looks as you made it. When it is off, the game grades your video too.
  • Direct audio output (VideoAudioDirect, default off) — if the sound of a replaced cinematic still chops, turn this switch on. On some platforms this mode plays no sound, so it stays off by default.

CustomizerCinematic skins play their cutscene videos (the skin's VideoClip/ folder) in place. To turn one into a normal pack, use Convert on its card in the Pack Manager: it copies the videos into Videos/ (Installing Packs).

That's the whole feature

Name the file for the cinematic, encode it with the settings above, put it in Videos/, and click Apply. The next time that cinematic plays, your video plays in its place. When two packs have a video for one cinematic, the usual pack order decides.
