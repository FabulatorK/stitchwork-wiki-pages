A4 How Stitchwork Works

introcreator2 min read
For Stitchwork 0.9.0 · Last edited: 2026-10-19

Creator route · start small.
Making a pack takes more reading and testing than installing one.
Read this short primer, then pick one kind of asset for your first edit.

Replacement happens at load time

Stitchwork never edits the game's asset files. It catches the moment the game asks for an asset and answers with your file instead of the original. Turn the pack off and the game gets the original back. Intercept the request, swap the reply — that's the whole model, and why you never repack the game's files and your save is never at risk.

Under the hood (optional)

Stitchwork uses Harmony to hook the game's asset calls — sprite setters, Language.Get, audio playback. When the game touches an asset you've replaced, the hook swaps in your version. Stitchwork never edits the game's asset files; the swap is live-only.

Your working files

Stitchwork keeps your work beside the game, not inside the mod. Stitchwork/ in the game folder holds the files you make, and Packs/ holds the packs you install. Mod-manager updates never reach either folder. Move along the thread below to watch Stitchwork's folders appear, and pick any of them to see what it holds.

The Overview panel (2) shows your working folder. Browse… chooses another place, and Migrate now brings over files that an older version kept beside the DLL. The folder reference shows the layout inside a pack.

Making graphics? A preview of the two sprite systems

The full choice of editing workflows is in B0.

The two sprite systems

The most useful fact here: Silksong draws 2D art in two ways, and the way that it draws a piece of art decides where your file goes.

  • tk2d — almost every animated character (Hornet, enemies, bosses), packed into shared atlases. Files go under Sprites/[Collection]/[Material]/.
  • Texture2D ("T2D") — the trouble child, which does a bit of everything: menus, HUD, effects, ability art, some cinematic frames. It even animates some of it, with Unity's own Animator instead of tk2d. Files go under Sprites/T2D/ or Spritesheets/T2D/.

You needn't memorize it — the Dev Hub's Graphics tab (key 3) tells you. Its Animation mode shows tk2d; its T2D Textures and T2D Log modes show Texture2D. (Its fourth mode, Colors, holds the color rules: Colors.) When a replacement won't take, first ask: is it the other system?

One landmine that's caught every Silksong creator: the game calls Hornet "Knight" — a Hollow Knight holdover. Sprites named Knight_… are hers. (The glossary has more.)

Hot reload

Hot reload means that your changes show up in the game while it runs. Stitchwork works inside the running game, so you keep playing while you edit. To see a change, you never quit the game, launch it again or load your save again.

Stitchwork watches your working folder and your pack folders. Save a file. A watcher sees the change, and Stitchwork draws your file onto the live atlas a moment later:

edit PNG → save → watcher → reload → composite → on screen   (no restart)

Only a new Stitchwork version (the .dll) needs a restart. Pack content never does.

When packs disagree

Multiple packs, same assets: pack manager displays all packs as a list, the order decides the priority. Pack placed higher on that list wins, and the manager shows who took it. Two exceptions always win over packs, so your in-game edits are never silently lost: text in your working folder's Text/ (where the Text tab's editor saves), and color rules in its Recolor/ (where the Colors mode saves). These changes might get more updates before the 1.0 version release and will be consolidated then. Full rule in Priority & Conflicts.

What do you want to change?

  • A character or other art: B0 · Choosing a Workflow, then B1 · your first skin edit.
  • Dialogue or UI words: C1 · Text & Dialogue.
  • Sound effects or music: D1 · Audio.
  • A cinematic: E1 · Video.

Make one small replacement and check it in-game before going into full production mode with your project. Each guide takes you through the editing steps for its asset type. When you are ready to share, use Distributing Your Pack; you can leave publishing details until then.
