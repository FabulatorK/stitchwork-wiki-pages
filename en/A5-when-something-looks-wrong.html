A5 When Something Looks Wrong

introplayercreator6 min read
For Stitchwork 0.9.0 · Last edited: 2026-10-19

Foundations · everyone.
A skin doesn't show correctly?
Find most causes in 90 seconds with three debug questions.
The rare cases are in Troubleshooting.

You'll be able to: find the cause of "it's not working" without guesswork, by asking three questions in order — and know when to stop looking here and open the full reference.

First, breathe — nothing's broken

Stitchwork never writes to your save and never edits the game's asset files. Whatever you're seeing, the worst case is a replacement didn't apply — not damage. You can't corrupt anything from here. So this is never damage control; it's just finding which link in a short chain came loose.

The chain is: Stitchwork running → pack loaded → pack enabled → the right asset on screen. Let's tackle your issue in this sequence.

Question 1 — Is Stitchwork even running?

Look at the bottom-left corner. A small badge there, the status badge, indicates Stitchwork is loaded.

The badge can be gone while Stitchwork runs. The eye on the badge turns on auto-hide: the badge hides at the left edge until the mouse comes near. The badge also hides during World Pick, and while another mod's interface, such as DebugMod's, shows. The Status badge switch in Settings › General turns it off completely. So look for a second sign: move the mouse to the top edge of the screen for the pull tabs, or find the line Stitchwork is loaded! in BepInEx/LogOutput.log. No badge, no pull tabs, and no log line: no Stitchwork, and nothing downstream can work.

The overwhelmingly common cause: the Patchwork mod is also installed. Stitchwork is a fork of it, the two fight over sprite loading, so Stitchwork steps aside and goes inert with a notice on the main menu. Remove Patchwork.dll from BepInEx/plugins/ and it wakes up. (Using the Architect-Silksong editor? It coexists fine — only a standalone Patchwork.dll trips this.)

If it's not Patchwork, it's almost always: BepInEx isn't installed (no BepInEx/core/BepInEx.dll), or the DLL is in the wrong place (it must sit one level under plugins/, e.g. plugins/StitchworkRoot/Stitchwork.dll — not loose in plugins/, not buried deeper).

Customizer and its variants can also cause problems, mostly with a skin that was made for Customizer. Stitchwork does not support running beside Customizer: the two mods change the same game sprites with similar hooks. The skins still work in Stitchwork: Getting Started says what to do.

The built-in second opinion: key 2 opens the Dashboard as the Overview panel. Near its top is the Health fold — a boot self-check covering version pairing, install files, and internal bridges. Its header reads "n/m checks pass" when Stitchwork and your game version agree. In amber, it reads "n of m checks need attention": click the fold to list the checks. That list, quoted in a report, usually answers the question before anyone opens a log.

Question 2 — Did the pack load, and is it ON?

These are two different checks. The second one is easy to miss.

  1. Loaded? Open the Pack Manager: press 1, or click the Packs pull tab at the top edge, or click the status badge. Look for the pack's card. (The badge counts only enabled packs, so it can't tell you this.) Added the pack while the game ran? Press Rescan in the top row of the panel. Still no card? The pack's files sit one level too high: there is no Stitchwork/ folder around its content. A pack must land as <Game folder>/Packs/{pack}/Stitchwork/ or …/BepInEx/plugins/{author}-{pack}/Stitchwork/. See Installing Packs. A pack that is nested one level too deep shows a card, but it loads nothing. The Pack Doctor reports "content is nested one level too deep" for it.
  2. Enabled? New packs remain off by design. Since Stitchwork supports loading multiple packs, a new pack shouldn't rewrite your game without explicit permission. Click the switch on the pack's card, then click Apply. Wait for the progress plate at the bottom of the screen. When it's done, your pack is on.

Question 3 — Are you looking at the right thing?

The pack is working; you're just not seeing it yet.

  • Wrong frame. Animated characters only show your replacement when that frame is on screen. Replaced an attack pose? You won't see it standing still. Do the action that has the replacement art. Walk through the animation library: Open Graphics (key 3) in Animation mode. Insert pauses the animation, PageUp / PageDown step one frame, and Delete freezes the object. See The Animation Mode.
  • Wrong sprite system. Silksong draws 2D art two ways (tk2d vs Texture2D), and a file in the wrong system's folder simply isn't found. If a replacement won't take, is it the other system? — see How Stitchwork Works.
  • Wrong scene. If the pack has certain conditions, it only fires where they say. Walk into the scene it targets — and confirm the scene name (the second line of the status badge shows it) matches what the condition expects. See Conditional Activation.

When to leave this page

If the three questions did not find the cause, open Troubleshooting. It carries the edge cases (audio first-play quirks, off-screen windows, the known cosmetic limitations) and, at the bottom, how to file a bug report that can actually be acted on. The single most useful thing you can attach is BepInEx/LogOutput.log — without it, anyone helping you is guessing at best. To find it, open Settings (key 0) › Advanced and click Show in folder. The BepInEx folder opens, with the log selected on Windows and macOS, so you can drag it into a message. Copy the log before you start the game again: each start writes a new log.

Do Stitchwork's panels stop responding, or disappear? Send the log. When a panel goes missing, a click does not reach a panel, or Stitchwork has an error, Stitchwork writes a snapshot of its interface to the log by itself.
