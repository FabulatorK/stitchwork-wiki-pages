B3 Porting Prior Work — Spritesheets & CKC

intermediatecreator7 min read
For Stitchwork 0.9.1 · Last edited: 2026-10-22

Sprites · for creators with existing work.
Bring a Custom Knight Creator skin, or another sheet-based skin, into Stitchwork unchanged.
For new art, the routes in B0 work better, and this page says why.

You'll be able to: load a finished whole-sheet skin through the Spritesheets/ tier, know the one rule that keeps it working, and know where the sheet path's limits are — so you can decide what to port as-is and what to rebuild.

Custom Knight Creator, and why we like it

Custom Knight Creator (CKC) is a standalone skinning tool — first built for the original Hollow Knight, since ported to work with Silksong assets. It runs outside the game and covers much of the same ground Stitchwork does in-game. We welcome CKC users and CKC work; Stitchwork is not a replacement for it, and packs made from CKC output are first-class citizens here.

CKC's real insight is one we share: don't draw directly on atlas sheets. CKC shows you the specific sprites of each animation, previews the animation, and lets you check that the frames flow smoothly together. Drawing straight onto a sheet gives scattered crops and no way to line frames up against each other, so smooth animation is hard. Many older skins were drawn straight onto sheets, before tools such as CKC existed. Stitchwork's in-game answer to the same problem is the Animation Canvas; CKC's is its preview loop. Either way, the sheet is an output format, not a drawing surface.

So, to be plain: this page does not teach you to dump a sheet, paint on it, and ship it. It shows how to load finished sheet-based work. For new art, use the routes in B0.

The one rule: match the sheet exactly

Silksong draws 2D art in two ways, tk2d and T2D (B4 shows how to tell them apart). A whole-sheet replacement goes in place of the original atlas. The game still reads each sprite from the same coordinates as before. So a ported sheet must keep:

  • The same shape — for T2D, the same width and height, to the pixel. For tk2d, the same width-to-height ratio: the original size, or a uniform scale of it (exactly 2× both ways, say). A tk2d sheet with a different ratio is refused, and the log says so.
  • The same layout — every sprite in its original rectangle. Nothing moved, resized, or repacked.

Break the layout and sprites read from the wrong pixels — art smeared across frames, pieces of one sprite bleeding into another. CKC output already matches, because CKC works from the real sheets.

A whole sheet cannot give one sprite more room than its slot. This is also true for a tk2d sheet at a uniform scale. To give one sprite more room, make that sprite an individual file with an anchor (Bigger Art: Anchors) on top of the sheet.

tk2d sheets: CKC output

For a tk2d collection (most animated characters — Hornet included, under Knight), a ported sheet goes to:

Spritesheets/{CollectionName}/{MaterialName}.png

For example, atlas2 is one of the Knight collection's materials (one atlas sheet):

Spritesheets/Knight/atlas2.png

Two notes:

  • There is no tk2d atlas dump, by design. Stitchwork does not give you a sheet to edit. Your sheet comes from CKC or from prior work, and Stitchwork loads it as it is.
  • Don't retouch the ported sheet to iterate. When a ported skin needs fixes or new art, do the new work with the in-game loop (B1) or the Animation Canvas — individual files layer on top of your sheet, so the port stays untouched. Those tools write to your working folder. If the sheet is in a pack, the fixes must go into that pack or a pack above it (Sheets and individual sprites compose).

T2D sheets — supported, but fragile across game updates

The T2D equivalent path also loads:

Spritesheets/T2D/{AtlasName}.png

({AtlasName} is the clean name that T2D Textures shows — Hornet, not the raw sactx-…-hash string. For a multi-page atlas, T2D Textures labels the pages Hornet-0, Hornet-1. These labels are not file names: Hornet-0.png does not match. The exception: two pages that share a size need the raw form, see B4.)

We do not recommend that you make or maintain T2D art at the sheet level. Unity packs T2D atlases, and a game update can rearrange the sprites on a sheet and change the texture's hash. After an update, one of three things happens to a ported T2D sheet:

  • Only the hash changed: the sheet still applies when the clean name, the page index (sactx-N) and the PNG's pixel size agree. The log notes a fuzzy match.
  • The size changed: the sheet is not applied, and the log shows a warning.
  • The layout changed at the same size: the sheet is applied, with wrong art in wrong slots and no error. Per-sprite files (Sprites/T2D/…, B4) key on the individual sprite and survive repacks; sheets don't.

If you have T2D sheet work, port it. At the next game update, rebuild it as per-sprite files. The Atlas button (the Dump row of the Preview panel, T2D Textures mode) writes the sheet to Dumps/. That image shows what is on a sheet and how much room each slot has. Use it as a reference, not as a template to ship.

One exception: a texture with no sprites, such as a particle texture, has nothing to split. For it, Edit (new from vanilla) writes the whole texture to Spritesheets/T2D/<name>.png, so the sheet path is the normal path there.

Converting a ported sheet to individual sprites

You can split a ported sheet into individual sprite files. In Utilities (7), open the Dumping panel. Click Convert sheets. Stitchwork writes each loaded whole-sheet replacement as sprite PNGs under Converted/ in your working folder, with the same folder layout as Sprites/. Move these files into a pack to use them with anchors, the Animation Canvas and Appliqué.

The switch Only sprites that differ from vanilla is on by default. It compares each sprite with the vanilla atlas and writes only the sprites that your sheet changed. A full CKC export has hundreds of frames, and most of them are vanilla. With the switch on, it converts to a small pack of only your art. The result has no conflicts over frames that you did not change, and each sprite can grow past its slot.

Load the sheets before you click Convert sheets. To load a sheet, go to a scene that uses its collection. Hornet's sheets are always loaded. Each run writes a new set of files. If Converted/ already has files, a dialog asks first. Back up, then convert moves them to Converted.backup-<date>/ beside it. Replace without a backup deletes them. A right click cancels, and nothing is written.

Sheets and individual sprites compose

You do not have to choose between a sheet and individual files. Within a material, a whole-sheet replacement is the base layer, and individual Sprites/ files draw on top of it. This is what makes porting practical: the CKC sheet sets everything, and every fix or improvement you make afterwards is an individual file stacked over it. The port never has to be re-exported.

This stacking works inside one pack (or inside your working folder). Across packs, priority decides: a sheet from a pack higher in the list covers the whole material, and the individual files of the packs below it don't show there. To layer your fixes over someone else's sheet, put your pack above theirs. See Priority & Conflicts.

Your working folder counts as the lowest source of all. Edit and Compile back write there, so while the ported sheet sits in an active pack, those fixes do not show on that material. Push them into the sheet's own pack (the Overview panel's Target pack: and Push), or into a pack above it. The COVERED fold in Packs shows a row for the sheet that hides them (sheet:… for a tk2d sheet). Hover a sheet: row: it says that the pack's sheet takes the whole material, and what to do.

Tips

  • Port, don't reconstruct. If you have the CKC export, use it as-is. A sheet rebuilt by hand from frame dumps has the problem of any sheet drawn by hand: you cannot line the frames up against each other. It also takes more work.
  • Keep your layered sources (.psd/.kra) in the pack folder if you like — Stitchwork ignores non-matching files, so editable sources ride along harmlessly.
  • White specks around the edges of your art? A sheet saved from Clip Studio Paint hides white under its transparent pixels. Clean sprite edges (CSP fix) in Settings › Rendering removes the specks when a sheet loads, and the Pack Doctor can clean the files on disk (B1 has the details).
