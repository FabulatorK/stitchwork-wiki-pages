B3 Porting Prior Work — Spritesheets & CKC

intermediatecreator8 min read
For Stitchwork 0.9.0 · Last edited: 2026-10-19

Sprites · for creators with existing work.
Bring a Custom Knight Creator skin, or another sheet-based skin, into Stitchwork unchanged.
For new art, the routes in B0 work better, and this page says why.

You'll be able to: load a finished full-sheet skin through the Spritesheets/ tier, know the one rule that keeps it working, and know where the sheet path's limits are — so you can decide what to port as-is and what to rebuild.

Custom Knight Creator, and why we like it

Custom Knight Creator (CKC) is a standalone skinning tool — first built for the original Hollow Knight, since ported to work with Silksong assets. It runs outside the game and covers much of the same ground Stitchwork does in-game. We welcome CKC users and CKC work; Stitchwork is not a replacement for it, and packs made from CKC output are first-class citizens here.

CKC's real insight is one we share: don't draw directly on atlas sheets. CKC shows you the specific sprites of each animation, previews the animation, and lets you check that the frames flow smoothly together. Drawing straight onto a sheet gives scattered crops and no way to line frames up against each other. That makes smooth animation hard. Many older skins were made this way, before these tools existed. Stitchwork's in-game answer to the same problem is the Animation Canvas; CKC's is its preview loop. Either way, the sheet is an output format, not a drawing surface.

So, to be plain: this page does not teach you to dump a sheet, paint on it, and ship it. For new art, the routes in B0 work better. This page exists because finished sheet-based work deserves a door.

The one rule: match the sheet exactly

A whole-sheet replacement is stamped in place over the original atlas — for tk2d it's blitted onto the atlas, for T2D it's loaded straight into the texture. The game's sprite definitions still point at the same coordinates they always did. So a ported sheet must keep:

  • The same shape — for T2D, the same width and height, to the pixel. For tk2d, the same width-to-height ratio: the original size, or a uniform scale of it (exactly 2× both ways, say). A tk2d sheet with a different ratio is refused, and the log says so.
  • The same layout — every sprite in its original rectangle. Nothing moved, resized, or repacked.

Break the layout and sprites read from the wrong pixels — art smeared across frames, pieces of one sprite bleeding into another. A tk2d sheet with the wrong shape does not load at all. CKC output satisfies this by construction, because CKC works from the real sheets.

Whole-sheet replacements also cannot grow the way individual sprites can. A ported sheet keeps the vanilla layout. A tk2d sheet can scale uniformly (see above), but one sprite cannot get more room than its slot. If one sprite needs more room, that one becomes an individual file with an anchor (Bigger Art: Anchors) layered on top.

tk2d sheets — the CKC door

For a tk2d collection (most animated characters — Hornet included, under Knight), a ported sheet goes to:

Spritesheets/{CollectionName}/{MaterialName}.png

For example, one of the Knight collection's atlas pages:

Spritesheets/Knight/atlas2.png

This tier exists to support exactly this porting path. Two honest notes that follow from that:

  • There is no tk2d atlas dump — by design. Stitchwork won't hand you a tk2d sheet as an editing template, because sheet-editing isn't a workflow we want to point anyone toward. Your sheet comes from CKC or from prior work; Stitchwork's job is to load it faithfully.
  • Don't retouch the ported sheet to iterate. When a ported skin needs fixes or new art, do the new work with the in-game loop (B1) or the Animation Canvas — individual files layer on top of your sheet (see below), so the port stays untouched. Those tools write to your working folder. If the sheet sits in a pack, the fixes must go into that pack or a pack above it (see below).

T2D sheets — supported, but fragile across game updates

The T2D equivalent path also loads:

Spritesheets/T2D/{AtlasName}.png

({AtlasName} is the clean name that T2D Textures shows — Hornet, not the raw sactx-…-hash string. The exception: two pages that share a size need the raw form, see B4.)

We no longer recommend authoring or maintaining T2D art at the sheet level at all, and the reason is mechanical: T2D atlases are packed by Unity, and a game update can rearrange the sprites on the sheet and change the texture's hash — part of which shows up in the name itself. A new hash alone does not break the match. The sheet still applies when the clean name, the page index (sactx-N) and the PNG's pixel size agree, and the log notes a fuzzy match. A sheet at a new size is not applied, and the log shows a warning. A re-layout at the same size is applied: wrong art in wrong slots, with no error. Per-sprite files (Sprites/T2D/…, B4) key on the individual sprite and survive repacks; sheets don't.

If you have existing T2D sheet work: port it, enjoy it, and treat the next game update as the moment to rebuild it as per-sprite files. The Atlas button, in the Dump row of the Preview panel (T2D Textures mode), remains genuinely useful — as a reference map of what lives on a sheet and how much room each slot has — just not as a template to ship.

One exception: a texture with no sprites, such as a particle texture, has nothing to split. For it, Edit (new from vanilla) writes the whole texture to Spritesheets/T2D/<name>.png, so the sheet path is the normal path there.

Converting a ported sheet to individual sprites

The porting door has an exit ramp. Utilities (7) → the Dumping panel → Convert sheets splits every currently loaded whole-sheet replacement into individual sprite PNGs under Converted/, mirroring the Sprites/ folder grammar — ready to move into a pack and use with the modern workflows (anchors, the animation canvas, Appliqué).

The switch Only sprites that differ from vanilla (on by default) is the point: it compares every sprite against the vanilla atlas and writes only the frames your sheet actually altered. A full CKC export — hundreds of frames, most of them untouched vanilla — converts to a minimal pack of just your art. Smaller pack, no false conflicts, and each sprite can now grow past its slot.

Two practical notes: sheets must be loaded when you press the button (visit a scene that uses the collection — Hornet's are always loaded), and each run writes a fresh set. If Converted/ already has files, a dialog asks first. Back up, then convert moves them to Converted.backup-<date>/ beside it. Replace without a backup deletes them. A right click cancels, and nothing is written.

Sheets and individual sprites compose

You don't have to choose globally. Within a material, a whole-sheet replacement is the base layer, and individual Sprites/ files draw on top of it. This is what makes porting practical: the CKC sheet sets everything, and every fix or improvement you make afterwards is an individual file stacked over it. The port never has to be re-exported.

This stacking works inside one pack (or inside your working folder). Across packs, priority decides: a sheet from a pack higher in the list covers the whole material, and the individual files of the packs below it don't show there. To layer your fixes over someone else's sheet, put your pack above theirs. See Priority & Conflicts.

Your working folder counts as the lowest source of all. Edit and Compile back write there, so while the ported sheet sits in an active pack, those fixes do not show on that material. Push them into the sheet's own pack (the Overview panel's Target pack: and Push), or into a pack above it. The COVERED fold in Packs shows a row for the sheet that hides them (sheet:… for a tk2d sheet). Hover a sheet: row: it says that the pack's sheet takes the whole material, and what to do.

Tips

  • Port, don't reconstruct. If you have the CKC export, use it as-is. A sheet rebuilt by hand from frame dumps has the same alignment problem, and it takes more work.
  • Keep your layered sources (.psd/.kra) in the pack folder if you like — Stitchwork ignores non-matching files, so editable sources ride along harmlessly.
  • One sheet = every sprite on it. A sheet swap touches the entire atlas, including frames you didn't mean to change. Individual files on top are how you take back control of specific sprites.
  • White specks around the edges of your art? A sheet saved from Clip Studio Paint hides white under its transparent pixels. Clean sprite edges (CSP fix) in Settings › Rendering removes the specks when a sheet loads, and the Pack Doctor can clean the files on disk (B1 has the details).
